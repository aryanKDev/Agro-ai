DRIP IRRIGATION GUIDE FOR VEGETABLE CROPS — INDIA
===================================================
Source: Central Soil and Water Conservation Research & Training Institute (CSWCRTI), Dehradun
Category: Irrigation Management | Under: Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY)

---

SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION TO DRIP IRRIGATION

Drip irrigation (also called trickle or micro-irrigation) delivers water directly to the plant root zone through a network of pipes, tubes, valves, and emitters at a slow, steady rate. This method is particularly suited for vegetable crops where water use efficiency is critical.

Benefits over Flood/Furrow Irrigation:
- Water saving: 40–60% less water consumption compared to surface irrigation
- Yield increase: 20–50% higher yield due to optimal soil moisture
- Fertilizer efficiency: fertigation allows 30–40% reduction in fertilizer use
- Disease reduction: dry foliage reduces fungal disease incidence
- Labour saving: automated timers reduce irrigation labour by 70%
- Weed reduction: only root zone wetted, not inter-row spaces

SECTION 2: DRIP SYSTEM COMPONENTS

2.1 MAIN COMPONENTS
1. Water source (well, borehole, canal, reservoir)
2. Pump unit (centrifugal pump 2–5 HP for 1–2 acres)
3. Filter unit:
   - Sand/gravel filter (removes organic debris)
   - Disc filter (removes mineral particles >130 micron)
   - Screen filter (final polishing — 120–200 mesh)
4. Pressure gauge and regulator (maintain 1.0–2.5 kg/cm²)
5. Fertilizer injection unit (venturi injector or fertilizer tank)
6. Main PVC pipe (75–90 mm diameter)
7. Sub-main HDPE pipe (40–63 mm diameter)
8. Laterals (drip tape or inline emitter tube — 12–16 mm)
9. Emitters/drippers: 2 LPH (litres per hour) for vegetables

2.2 EMITTER SELECTION FOR VEGETABLE CROPS
- Tomato, Brinjal, Capsicum: 2 LPH inline dripper at 30–45 cm spacing
- Onion, Garlic: Drip tape with 20 cm emitter spacing
- Cucumber, Melons: 4 LPH drippers at 60 cm spacing
- Potato: Drip tape at 30 cm emitter spacing

SECTION 3: WATER REQUIREMENT AND SCHEDULING

3.1 TOMATO WATER REQUIREMENTS (per acre per day)
- Establishment (0–15 DAT): 600–800 litres/day (2–3 irrigations × 20 min)
- Vegetative growth (15–45 DAT): 1000–1500 litres/day
- Flowering and fruit set (45–75 DAT): 1500–2000 litres/day (critical stage — never stress)
- Fruit development (75–100 DAT): 1800–2500 litres/day
- Pre-harvest maturation (100+ DAT): 800–1000 litres/day (reduce for quality)

3.2 IRRIGATION SCHEDULING PRINCIPLES
Method 1 — Pan Evaporation Method:
Daily irrigation (litres/acre) = Ep × Kc × Area × correction factor
Where: Ep = pan evaporation (mm/day), Kc = crop coefficient

Tomato Kc values:
- Initial stage (0–30 DAT): Kc = 0.6
- Development (30–70 DAT): Kc = 0.7–1.15 (increasing)
- Mid-season (70–100 DAT): Kc = 1.15
- Late season (100–120 DAT): Kc = 0.70–0.85

Method 2 — Tensiometer Method:
- Install tensiometer at 20 cm and 40 cm depth
- Irrigate when tensiometer reads 20–30 centibars (cbar) for tomato
- Do not allow soil to dry beyond 40 cbar — causes blossom end rot

Method 3 — Fixed Schedule (Kharif Season):
- Morning: 6:00–7:00 AM — 30 minutes (avoid evening irrigation — promotes fungal diseases)
- Frequency: Daily during summer, every other day during monsoon

SECTION 4: FERTIGATION THROUGH DRIP IRRIGATION

Fertigation Schedule for Drip-Irrigated Tomato:

Stage 1 (Week 1–2, Establishment):
- 19:19:19 Water Soluble Fertilizer (WSF): 2 kg/acre dissolved in tank
- Humic acid: 1 kg/acre

Stage 2 (Week 3–5, Vegetative Growth):
- 19:19:19 WSF: 3 kg/acre per week
- Calcium Nitrate (15.5-0-0+26.5 Ca): 2 kg/acre per week
- Magnesium Sulfate: 1 kg/acre per week

Stage 3 (Week 6–8, Flowering):
- Mono Ammonium Phosphate (12:61:0) MAP: 3 kg/acre per week
- Mono Potassium Phosphate (0:52:34) MKP: 1 kg/acre per week
- Boron foliar spray: 0.2% solution (not through drip)

Stage 4 (Week 9–12, Fruit Development):
- 0:0:50 Potassium Sulfate (SOP): 3 kg/acre per week
- Calcium Nitrate: 2 kg/acre per week

Stage 5 (Week 13–15, Harvest):
- Potassium Nitrate (13:0:45): 2 kg/acre per week
- Reduce overall input, maintain K for fruit coloring

Fertigation Rules:
1. Always flush system with clean water 10 minutes before and after fertigation
2. Do not mix incompatible fertilizers (e.g., Calcium Nitrate + Phosphate fertilizers) — apply separately
3. Maintain filter cleanliness — clean filters weekly during fertigation
4. Never use urea in drip systems with hard water (scaling risk)
5. Keep fertilizer tank filled to 60–70% capacity for proper mixing

SECTION 5: GOVERNMENT SUBSIDY FOR DRIP IRRIGATION — INDIA

5.1 PMKSY — Per Drop More Crop (MI) Component
National subsidy for micro-irrigation systems:

Small and Marginal Farmers (landholding ≤ 2 ha):
- Subsidy: 55% of unit cost
- Unit cost approved by GoI: ₹80,000–₹1,20,000 per hectare (varies by state)
- Effective farmer cost: ₹36,000–₹54,000 per hectare

Other Farmers (landholding > 2 ha):
- Subsidy: 45% of unit cost
- Effective farmer cost: ₹44,000–₹66,000 per hectare

Additional State Subsidy:
- Several states (Maharashtra, Gujarat, Telangana, Karnataka) provide additional 10–25% subsidy
- Maharashtra: Total subsidy up to 80% for SC/ST farmers under Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar Krishi Swavalamban Yojana

5.2 HOW TO APPLY FOR SUBSIDY
1. Register on PMKSY official portal: pmksy.gov.in
2. Obtain Farm ID (FID) from local agriculture department
3. Apply through Cooperative Bank or Agriculture Department with land records
4. Purchase system from PMKSY-approved manufacturers only
5. Department inspects installation before releasing subsidy amount

SECTION 6: MAINTENANCE AND TROUBLESHOOTING

Common Problems and Solutions:

Problem: Emitters clogging
- Cause: Suspended particles, algae, calcium deposits, or root intrusion
- Solution: Flush laterals weekly; inject chlorine (4–5 ppm) monthly; use anti-root emitters

Problem: Uneven water distribution
- Cause: Pressure variation or clogged emitters
- Solution: Check pressure gauge; use pressure-compensating emitters on slopes

Problem: Filter clogging
- Cause: Heavy silt in water source
- Solution: Clean sand filter daily during summer; install settling tank before pump

Problem: Lateral damage by rodents
- Cause: Mice chewing on lateral tape
- Solution: Bury laterals 5–8 cm deep; use rodenticide bait stations near field

System Life Expectancy:
- Drip laterals (tape): 3–5 seasons
- Inline emitter tube: 5–8 seasons
- HDPE sub-mains and mains: 15–20 years
- Filters and pump: 10–15 years with annual maintenance
