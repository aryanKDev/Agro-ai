MONSOON AND WEATHER ADVISORY FOR FARMERS — INDIA
==================================================
Source: India Meteorological Department (IMD) & ICAR Agricultural Weather Division
Category: Weather Advisory | Season: Kharif, Rabi, Zaid

---

SECTION 1: KHARIF SEASON (JUNE–OCTOBER) ADVISORY

1.1 MONSOON SOWING CALENDAR
- Kerala / Karnataka: June 1–10
- Maharashtra / MP / Chhattisgarh: June 10–20
- UP / Bihar / Jharkhand: June 15–25
- Punjab / Haryana / Rajasthan: July 1–10

Do NOT sow before 50–75 mm of rainfall over 2–3 consecutive days. Dry sowing leads to poor germination.

Kharif crops and sowing windows:
- Paddy (transplanted): June 15 – July 15
- Maize: June 15 – July 10
- Soybean: June 20 – July 5
- Groundnut: June 15 – July 10
- Tomato (Kharif planting): June 15 – July 15 (nursery May–June)
- Onion (Kharif): June – July

1.2 EL NIÑO VS LA NIÑA ADVISORY
El Niño Years (Deficit Monsoon):
- Delay sowing by 10–15 days; use drought-tolerant varieties
- Reduce water-intensive crops (paddy); shift to millets, pulses
- Prepare supplemental irrigation for critical stages (flowering, grain fill)

La Niña Years (Excess Monsoon):
- Dig drainage channels in field before monsoon onset
- High disease pressure — apply preventive Mancozeb 2.5 g/L sprays
- Risk of flooding and soil erosion — use ridges and raised beds

1.3 WATERLOGGING MANAGEMENT
If field is waterlogged:
1. Immediately create 30–45 cm drainage channels every 5–6 rows
2. Remove stagnant water within 24 hours (root rot begins at 48 hours)
3. After draining: soil drench with Trichoderma viride 5 g/L
4. Spray Urea 2% foliar (leaching causes nitrogen deficiency)
5. Preventive spray: Mancozeb 2.5 g/L for post-waterlogging fungal control

1.4 HIGH HUMIDITY DISEASE ALERT
Disease risk is HIGH when:
- Relative Humidity > 85% for 3+ consecutive days
- Leaf wetness > 6 hours at 20–28°C → Early/Late Blight in tomato/potato
- Temperature 18–25°C + rain → Downy Mildew in cucurbits
- Humid nights followed by sunny days → Powdery Mildew

Action: Apply Mancozeb or Chlorothalonil within 24 hours of first symptoms.

SECTION 2: RABI SEASON (NOVEMBER–MARCH) ADVISORY

2.1 RABI SOWING CALENDAR
- Wheat (North India): October 25 – November 25 (optimum)
- Mustard: October 15 – November 15
- Chickpea: October 20 – November 20
- Potato: October 1 – October 30
- Tomato (Rabi): October–November transplanting

Late wheat sowing (after December 15) reduces yield by 30–40 kg/ha per day of delay.

2.2 WINTER FOG ADVISORY (December–February, North India)
Fog creates prolonged leaf wetness — HIGH DISEASE RISK:
- Early blight and late blight in tomato/potato
- Botrytis grey mould in chickpea
- Powdery mildew in mustard, wheat

Preventive sprays during fog season:
- Mancozeb 2.5 g/L or Copper oxychloride 3.0 g/L — every 7–10 days
- Sulfur dust 30 kg/ha — for powdery mildew in mustard
- Delay irrigation during fog; no overhead irrigation

2.3 FROST PROTECTION (Temperature < 3°C)
Action before frost (based on IMD forecast):
1. Light irrigation before expected frost — wet soil radiates heat
2. Smoke generation (windward side of field)
3. Cover nurseries with agro-net or polythene overnight
4. Spray Potassium Nitrate 0.5% — improves frost tolerance
5. Spray Kaolin clay 5% — reflective coating protects from radiation frost

SECTION 3: SUMMER SEASON HEAT STRESS MANAGEMENT

3.1 HEAT EFFECTS ON CROPS
- Temperature > 35°C during flowering → pollen sterility, flower drop
- Temperature > 40°C → complete crop failure
- Tomato optimal temperature: 21–27°C (day), 15–20°C (night)

3.2 HEAT MITIGATION
- Irrigate early morning (5–7 AM) or late evening — NOT during peak heat
- Use 30–50% shade nets — reduces temperature by 3–5°C
- Black polyethylene mulch — reduces soil temperature by 4–6°C
- Spray Kaolin clay 5% — reflects solar radiation
- Potassium Nitrate foliar 0.5% — strengthens cell membranes under heat

Heat-tolerant varieties for tomato: Arka Rakshak, Pusa Sheetal
Heat-tolerant onion: Bhima Super, Arka Kalyan

SECTION 5: CLIMATE-SMART AGRICULTURE PRACTICES

1. Zero-tillage / Minimum Tillage — saves ₹1,500–2,000/acre fuel cost
2. System of Rice Intensification (SRI) — 25–30% water saving
3. Farm Ponds — harvest rainwater for supplemental irrigation
4. Crop Diversification — intercropping reduces weather-related losses
5. Agroforestry — border trees reduce wind damage, provide shade
