ICAR TOMATO DISEASE MANAGEMENT GUIDE
=====================================
Source: Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR), National Horticulture Research Development Foundation
Category: Plant Disease Management | Crop: Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum)

---

SECTION 1: EARLY BLIGHT (Alternaria Blight)
Causative Organism: Alternaria solani (Fungal pathogen)

1.1 SYMPTOMS OF EARLY BLIGHT
- Small, dark brown to black lesions (2-3 mm) appear on lower, older leaves first
- Lesions enlarge and develop characteristic concentric rings (target-board pattern)
- Affected areas turn yellow (chlorotic halo) around the lesion
- Severely infected leaves turn yellow, wither, and fall off (defoliation)
- On stems: dark, sunken, elongated lesions — can girdle the stem and kill the plant
- On fruits: dark, leathery, sunken lesions near the stem end; white to gray fungal growth visible in moist conditions
- Disease progresses upward from older to younger leaves

1.2 DISEASE CYCLE AND FAVORABLE CONDITIONS
- Pathogen survives in infected crop debris and soil for 12–16 months
- Spreads by wind, rain splash, contaminated tools, and infected seed
- Optimal temperature for infection: 24–29°C with relative humidity above 90%
- Short wet periods (even 2–3 hours of leaf wetness) trigger infection
- Common during Kharif (rainy) season and winter fog periods in North India

1.3 CHEMICAL CONTROL OF EARLY BLIGHT
Recommended fungicides and dosage (per litre of water):
- Mancozeb 75% WP — 2.5 g/L water (most effective, broad-spectrum)
- Chlorothalonil 75% WP — 2.0 g/L water
- Copper oxychloride 50% WP — 3.0 g/L water
- Iprodione 50% WP — 1.0 g/L water
- Azoxystrobin 23% SC — 1.0 mL/L water (systemic)
- Tebuconazole 25.9% EC — 1.0 mL/L water

Spray schedule: Begin spraying at first symptom appearance. Repeat every 7–10 days. Rotate fungicide groups to prevent resistance.

1.4 BIOLOGICAL CONTROL
- Trichoderma viride or T. harzianum: 4 g/L as foliar spray or soil application
- Pseudomonas fluorescens: 10 g/L as foliar spray — inhibits Alternaria germination
- Neem oil 3000 ppm: 5 mL/L water — effective as preventive spray

1.5 PREVENTION AND CULTURAL PRACTICES
- Use certified, disease-free seeds — treat with Thiram 75% WP at 3 g/kg seed
- Select resistant varieties: Arka Rakshak, Arka Samrat, Punjab Chhuhara, Pusa Rohini
- Maintain plant spacing: 60 cm × 45 cm for good air circulation
- Avoid overhead irrigation; use drip irrigation to keep foliage dry
- Remove and destroy infected plant debris immediately after harvest
- Practice crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops for 2–3 years
- Apply balanced fertilization — avoid excessive nitrogen which increases susceptibility
- Stake plants to improve air flow and reduce humidity around foliage
- Mulch soil to prevent rain splash of soil-borne spores

SECTION 2: LATE BLIGHT (Phytophthora Blight)
Causative Organism: Phytophthora infestans (Oomycete pathogen)

2.1 SYMPTOMS OF LATE BLIGHT
- Water-soaked, irregular, pale green to dark brown patches on leaves
- White cotton-like sporulation on the undersurface of leaves in moist conditions
- Rapid spread — entire plant can collapse within 3–5 days in humid conditions
- Brown, firm lesions on fruits; affected fruits become inedible
- Lesions on stems are brown and can cause stem break

2.2 CHEMICAL CONTROL OF LATE BLIGHT
- Metalaxyl-M 4% + Mancozeb 64% WP (Ridomil Gold): 2.5 g/L — most effective
- Dimethomorph 50% WP: 1.0 g/L water
- Cymoxanil 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP: 3.0 g/L water
- Fosetyl-Al 80% WP: 2.5 g/L water
- Propamocarb hydrochloride 72.2% SL: 2.0 mL/L water

2.3 PREVENTION OF LATE BLIGHT
- Grow resistant varieties: Arka Vikas, Pusa Gaurav, Himsona
- Avoid excessive irrigation during cool, humid weather
- Destroy volunteer tomato plants and potato crops nearby (alternate hosts)
- Apply prophylactic sprays before disease onset during high-risk periods (monsoon)

SECTION 3: TOMATO LEAF CURL VIRUS (ToLCV)
Causative Agent: Tomato Leaf Curl New Delhi Virus (TLCNDV) — transmitted by whitefly (Bemisia tabaci)

3.1 SYMPTOMS
- Upward or downward curling of leaflets
- Reduction in leaf size; leaves become thick and leathery
- Stunted plant growth; flower drop; no fruit set in severe cases
- Yellowing of younger leaves

3.2 MANAGEMENT
- Control whitefly vector with: Imidacloprid 17.8% SL (0.5 mL/L), Thiamethoxam 25% WG (0.3 g/L)
- Reflective silver mulch repels whitefly
- Install yellow sticky traps (20/acre) for monitoring
- Use virus-resistant varieties: Arka Rakshak, TH-1, Syngenta Abhilash
- Remove and destroy infected plants immediately to prevent spread

SECTION 4: BACTERIAL WILT
Causative Organism: Ralstonia solanacearum

4.1 SYMPTOMS
- Sudden wilting of entire plant, often without yellowing
- Vascular browning when stem is cut cross-sectionally
- Bacterial ooze visible when stem cut placed in water

4.2 MANAGEMENT
- No effective chemical cure once infected — prevention is critical
- Soil solarization: cover moist soil with transparent polyethylene for 4–6 weeks
- Soil drench with Copper oxychloride 0.3% or Streptomycin sulfate 200 ppm
- Crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops for 3–4 years
- Resistant rootstocks for grafting: IIHR-Sel-49 (Solanum torvum)
- Avoid waterlogging; maintain well-drained soil conditions

---

SECTION 5: INTEGRATED DISEASE MANAGEMENT (IDM) FOR TOMATO
1. Seed treatment before sowing with Thiram (3g/kg) or Carbendazim (2g/kg)
2. Seedling tray dip in Trichoderma viride (5g/L) for 30 minutes before transplanting
3. Soil application of Trichoderma at 2.5 kg/acre mixed with FYM at transplanting
4. First preventive spray at 15 days after transplanting with Mancozeb 2.5 g/L
5. Continue spray schedule every 10 days during monsoon and fog periods
6. Scout fields twice weekly — early detection reduces losses by 80%
7. Maintain field sanitation — remove plant debris and weeds
8. Apply recommended dose of fertilizers — excess nitrogen increases disease
